# Short form: Shorts, Reels, TikTok (9:16)

1080 × 1920, 30 fps. One idea, 20–30 s.

## Structure that worked

1. **Hook, second 0** — the strongest image and the strongest sentence together, no logo, no
   intro card, no "hi". A figure, a surprising claim, a lived scene.
2. **The tension** — why it matters to the viewer (their money, their street, their time).
3. **The one idea**, shown with real photos or real screens, one gesture per sentence.
4. **The turn** — a short formula or question that sets up the answer (a recurring series
   catchphrase works: it becomes the jingle of the series).
5. **The product moment** — shown *inside a phone*, not as a logo. ("We never see the app" was the
   first feedback on a version that only showed the logo.)
6. **CTA** — short, spoken, names the platforms: "free, on iPhone and Android". On screen: the
   official App Store / Google Play badges, never stretched, recoloured or redrawn.
7. **End card** — the logo, held **≥ 2.5 s**, and it is **the last frame**: a short loops, and
   without a final logo the loop restarts on the first image as if nothing was said.
8. **An engagement question** in the caption or at the end ("Is there one in your street?").

## Length

- 20–30 s. Episodes of 35–46 s retained less on the same series and audience.
- One idea per short. A second idea is a second short.
- If the voice ends early, extend the end card, never compress the animation.

## Subtitles

- Always on: most people watch muted.
- At the bottom, inside the safe zone (nothing important above y = 220 or below y ≈ 1540 on
  1920; side margin 60 px). Max ≈ 22 characters per line.
- **Never over a figure, a face, or a dark photo.** We moved them lower after they covered the key
  number. Turn them off on shots where a giant word already carries the sentence.
- Words light up with the voice (word timings from the voice alignment).

## Visual style

- **Propose 3+ styles as short mock-ups before choosing**, on the same opening. One proposal
  copied from a well-known channel was rejected as "too similar to <channel>": take mechanics from
  references, never their look.
- **Real photos make shorts credible.** Illustrations complement them; they don't replace them.
- **One accent colour per role**, one highlight mechanism repeated across the series.
- Figures roll or land; they are not faded in.
- Photos are never scaled beyond their native resolution: show a smaller rounded card rather than
  a blurry full-bleed image.

## Series

- A series = one template + one data file per episode (photos, overlays, cues). Only the script,
  the photos and the data change; the motion, the sound design and the end card are shared.
- Batch-produce: script all episodes → validate all texts at once → voices → renders → one contact
  sheet per batch (first frame / middle / last frame of every episode side by side).
- Each long-form chapter can become a short (see [long-form](long-form.md#recycling)).
